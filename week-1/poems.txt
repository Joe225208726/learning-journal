SHE THINKS OF THE EARTH AS A SINGLE STONE

Street lights bend and wrap themselves around her.
Pavements fold up and smother her.
She thinks of the earth as a single stone.
She warns: We will be killed by our own.

Birch trees in the shadow of half-finished tower blocks.
The moon’s orbit shifts. She predicts floods.
A burst pipeline will set fire to the sea.
The stupid will deny the plague.

The old writer twists the strands of memory
in her braided grey hair, reads her poems
into the machine, slowly, softly. She settles
into a rhythm of touch and sound.

Wind rattles the windows of the white room,
bringing rain after weeks of heat. She knows
no one will listen and knows it doesn’t matter.
She draws the black curtains.

Once I saw a white raven – the poet leans close,
closer to the machine. Once, yes, I did, she says.
It was sacred, it was alone, it was fierce, it saw
the river, the parallel lines of vines. It saw us.

*** ***

Could you ever write a novel? asks the woman at dinner.
Not to me, you understand. I am just an empty chair.
Her companion frowns, does not answer.
I wonder if he’s asking himself Why is she asking this?
Why is she asking me? Is she waiting for an answer,
any answer, in order to tell me about the novel
she’s writing, or planning to write, one day
when the talking stops. Yes, I can see him
considering all of this, before he says No.
Just a flat, even, disinterested No that’s neither
judgement nor invitation to continue.
With a fork, she pushes her seafood risotto
around the plate, sibs her Chablis.
She looks out to where wind ruffles the tips of
the long grass, turns the leaves on the poplars.

*** ***

The poet answers her door, steps back to let me in.
Front porch, she says. We go through the house
from where I’ve parked at the back on the hard ground
beneath the empty washing line. Brain cancer, she says,
pointing to my old friend, sitting in a wicker chair.
She leaves us to it. He reaches out a hand, sideways,
without turning his body. My mind, he says,
is gathering witnesses, compiling evidence
for me to defend myself on the day to come.
I have brought a photograph of us back around ’74.
There we are, sprawled by some lobster pots
near the steps of the Bay Hotel, looking vaguely
at the camera as if it’s a conversation interrupted.
Who took the photo? Who else was there?
Neither of us can remember but there we are,
and behind us a pregnant, dark-haired woman, hands
folded over her bump, head turned to the side,
a flowered smock over her long blue dress.
Beside her, an empty wicker chair.

*** ***

Inside the poet is talking once again into the machine.
I hear her whispering: What do you do? Turn to drink?
Maybe a little love here and there, or an inclination towards it?
Awkwardness. I am out of practice. Thoughts and words
struggle to match. I break the words into sound patterns.
There is nothing here, there is nothing here.
It’s all been done before, it’s all been done before.

Hsuan Hua fasted for thirty-five days throughout the Cuban Missile Crisis.
Seventy-five per cent of British children spend less time outdoors than inmates of British prisons.

The poor sell the night’s full chamber pots to the tannery.
Urine is good for cleansing skins.
Hence piss-poor or, worse, not having a pot to piss in.

To calculate the weight of a pig
Measure the length of the pig from ear to base of tail in inches.
Measure the girth of the pig behind its front legs in inches.
Multiply girth by girth by length.
Divide by 400 to get weight in pounds.

The point of the lost cat is that it may not consider itself lost.

In the coffee shop by the market square on a cold day
I heard a woman tell her friend: We were lovers once.
Her friend said, after a pause: It didn’t come to anything?
A bus passed by. The coffee shop began to fill up.
Two mothers pushing buggies, an old man in pain.

The wind whips sand into the car park
where a woman, huddled, shouts into a mobile.
The sea does its winter best to send us inland.
Either the wind or the voice in my head bellows
What is it you think you possess in this world?

*** ***

Somewhere south of here people flee the fighting.
They cling to trucks, pile on the roof of a bus.
Many, too weary to run, walk at the side of the road.
The earth shakes. They don’t have long.

*** ***

We find the foothills where berries grow
and lizards lie on yellow rocks, where
lines of men shovel melted tar, ignore
each other and us. We stop to pick figs.
An aproned woman sits in a doorway
shelling peas and scolding a dog.
Higher and higher, until we pause again
and in the far haze we glimpse a wave
out on the bright sea, a wave too big,
far too big, rolling in, gathering itself,
swelling, steepening, toppling, rising again.
